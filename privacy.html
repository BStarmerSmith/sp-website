Who we are

Suzy Parker Games Limited (company number 16522095, registered office 36 Harvey Street, Folkestone, Kent, CT20 1LE) makes the games in the Suzy Parker Games suite, including The Middle Bit. We are the data controller for all of them, for the purposes of UK and EU GDPR.

Questions about your data, or want to exercise any of the rights below? Email privacy@suzyparkergames.co.uk.

One account, every game

You have one Suzy Parker Games account, and it works across every game we make. Your email address, display name, password and subscription are shared across all of them. What you've played — your progress and your streaks — is kept per game. That's why there is one privacy policy rather than one per title.

That last row is worth being precise about, because it is kept indefinitely. It is recorded only when you play without an account, and only once the puzzle has ended. We record that it happened, which game it was, how it ended, the guesses you typed, how many hints you revealed and how long you spent. We attach no account, no session, no device identifier and no IP address — so it counts answers, not people: there is no identifier in it that could link two of these records to the same person, or trace one back to you. That is also why we can keep it indefinitely — there is nothing in it to identify anyone. It is the reason we can tell you how many puzzles our games have served without building a profile of anyone. If you play with an account, nothing of this kind is sent — your progress is simply saved to your account instead.

We keep ordinary server logs, as any website does. Every request leaves a line recording the IP address it came from, what was asked for, the browser or app that asked, and the page it came from; and when someone tries to sign in, change or reset a password, ask for another verification email, or delete an account, the email address used is recorded too — that is what makes a log useful if an account is attacked. We don't use them to build a profile, and we don't join them to what you've played.

Why we're allowed to do this

We handle your account, progress and subscription because we need to in order to provide the game you asked for. Where a consent form is required — in the UK and the EEA, and anywhere else with the same rule — we show personalised ads only if you said yes to it. Where no form is required, none is shown, and we rely instead on our legitimate interest in funding free games with advertising. Either way, if you want that changed, email privacy@suzyparkergames.co.uk and we'll act on it. We keep server logs, and the anonymous record of a finished puzzle described above — which includes the guesses you typed and how long you took — because we have a legitimate interest in keeping the games secure and knowing how much they're played and how people get on with them, and we keep the email you send us because we have a legitimate interest in answering it and in being able to show how we did.

What we don't do

  • We don't use third-party analytics. No Google Analytics, no tracking pixels, nothing of that kind, on our website or in our games.
  • We don't collect crash reports or diagnostics from your device.
  • We don't build an advertising profile of you. We don't sell personal data, and we never have.
  • We never see your card details. Payments are handled entirely by the companies listed below.

What our games keep on your device is only what they need to work: your sign-in tokens, your account details and progress so the game loads instantly, a few interface preferences, and — if you play without an account — that day's progress and a note of which puzzles have already been counted, so one answer isn't counted twice. On the web, clearing your browser data removes all of it. In our mobile apps your sign-in tokens are held in the device's own secure storage, which clearing data inside the app does not touch — signing out removes them, and so does uninstalling the app.

None of that is shared with anyone, with one exception we would rather name: if you play without an account, the anonymous record described in the table above is sent to us when the puzzle ends, and it includes the guesses you typed. It carries nothing that identifies you.

Who else sees your data

This is the complete list:

  • RevenueCat and Stripe — managing subscriptions and taking payment.
  • Apple and Google Play — purchases made inside our mobile apps.
  • Google AdSense and AdMob — the ads shown to players without Premium.
  • Amazon SES — delivering the emails we send you, such as password resets and deletion notices.
  • Email you send us. Mail to the addresses on this page is received and kept while we deal with it, by whoever hosts each mailbox.
  • Cloudflare — hosting this website.
  • Hetzner — the hosting company whose servers our games and your account data run on.
  • Google Fonts — the typeface this website and the web version of our games use. Your browser fetches it from Google, which means Google sees your IP address when a page loads. Our mobile apps use your device's own font and fetch nothing from Google.
  • Expo — delivering updates to our mobile apps. Each time you open one it checks Expo for a newer version, which means Expo sees your IP address, your platform and which version you're running.

Your account and game data sits on servers we run ourselves, rented from the hosting company named above. Some of the companies listed operate outside the UK and EEA; where that happens, standard contractual clauses or equivalent safeguards apply.

Before any ad loads, you may be asked up to two separate questions, and nothing is shown until they have finished.

On iPhone and iPad, Apple asks everyone. In our mobile apps, Apple's own permission prompt asks whether we may track you for advertising. Every player who is going to be shown ads is asked, wherever in the world they are, before an ad is ever requested. That prompt is Apple's, your answer is held by Apple, and we only ever see the result.

Where the law requires it, Google asks too. In the UK and the EEA, and anywhere else with the same rule, a consent form asks whether you want personalised advertising — Google's consent message in the web version of our games, and Google's own form inside the mobile apps. Where that form isn't required you won't see one, and ads can appear straight away. Apple's question is still asked on an iPhone or iPad either way.

Premium subscribers see no ads anywhere, and are asked neither question. Wherever you are, you can email privacy@suzyparkergames.co.uk to object to personalised advertising.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time from Settings. Here is exactly what happens.

Straight away: you're signed out everywhere and the account stops working. You can't sign in to it, on any of our games. We email you confirming the date it will be erased, which is 30 days later.

For those 30 days: nothing has been erased yet. Changed your mind? Email support@suzyparkergames.co.uk before that date and we'll restore the account with everything intact. We send one reminder three days before the deadline.

After 30 days: your account, progress, streaks, votes and subscription record are deleted from our database outright — removed, not hidden or flagged. This cannot be undone. Your email address becomes free to register with again, so you're welcome back any time.

Four things deliberately remain, and we'd rather say so than imply otherwise. The anonymous record of a puzzle being answered stays, because it contains nothing that identifies you — removing it would quietly rewrite how much our games have been played. If you created a puzzle or an invite code, that stays too, with the link to you removed. The server logs described above are not rewritten: lines already written keep the email address they recorded, so a sign-in or a password change from before you deleted the account can still be seen in them until those logs rotate out, which may be after the 30 days are up. Finally, email you sent us stays in the mailbox it arrived in; tell us you want it gone too and we'll delete it.

Payment records aren't ours to delete. Apple, Google, Stripe and RevenueCat each keep their own transaction records, for their own legal and tax reasons, under their own policies.

Backups. We back the database up every night and keep 30 nights' worth. Because erasure happens 30 days after you ask for it, and a backup taken the day before that can then sit for another 30 nights, data can persist in a backup for up to about 60 days from the moment you press delete. After that it rolls off and is gone.
